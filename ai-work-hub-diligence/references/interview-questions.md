# Questions That Produce Useful Answers

Use this reference for preliminary questions, founder/customer interviews and written company/FA follow-ups. The deliverable should help the user learn something that changes the next step, rather than make them redesign the interview on the spot. Match the user's language; the examples below are entirely fictional.

## Choose The Uncertainty Before The Question

Read the existing view and relevant answers first. Work out which unresolved explanation matters now: for example, whether faster growth comes from a repeatable product or unusually intensive service. Decide what kind of answer would move the judgment in either direction and who can actually know it. A founder can explain resource choices; a customer is better placed to describe purchasing and use. A factual financing clarification may only need a direct question.

Select the useful disagreements, not every missing fact. An early screen may only need enough information to choose the next conversation. Material commercial, technical, team or price uncertainties can all matter; this is not a standard set of categories to fill. If no plausible answer would change the next action, omit the question or keep it out of the main conversation.

## Write An Answerable Main Question

Anchor the main question in a particular event, task, choice or comparison that the recipient can reconstruct. Ask in ordinary spoken language. Introduce only the context needed to understand it; keep the analyst's hypothesis and source notes outside the sentence to be read aloud.

Give the recipient one clear focus at a time. This does not prohibit a naturally connected clarification, but do not compress the entire internal analysis into a question joined by commas. Split only where the answer actually determines what to ask next, rather than turning a long question into ten mandatory subquestions.

Prefer observed behavior to aspirational answers. Ask what happened at the last renewal before asking whether customers will renew; ask about a lost deal before accepting a general account of competitive advantage. A hypothetical can help explore a genuine future choice, but it is not evidence that the choice or outcome has occurred. Use neutral wording that leaves room for the strongest alternative explanation.

## Follow The Answer, Then Stop When It Is Useful Enough

Prepare branches for the few uncertainties whose answers imply different next steps. A branch names a response that would trigger the follow-up and the next useful distinction; it is not another topic list labeled “follow-up.” Do not force a decision tree onto straightforward questions.

For an ambiguous answer, ask for a concrete instance or clarify the disputed term. For a specific answer, move to its consequence, exception or comparison rather than repeating the original question. Carry earlier answers forward: if the company already said implementation takes six weeks, ask what determines the spread between faster and slower cases instead of asking its average again.

Decide what would be enough for the present stage. A coherent account of who did the work, why it was needed and what repeated in the next deployment may be enough to choose a product-lead conversation. It does not establish audited margins or prove industry-wide repeatability. Seek the cheapest useful clarification when a material contradiction remains; do not turn every answer into a request for contracts, raw data or tests. “Enough” is relative to the next decision, not an invented numerical pass mark.

## Make The Format Fit Its Use

**Live conversation.** Put the purpose and main questions where the interviewer can find them immediately. Add short conditional prompts or private rationale only where helpful. Rank the main line and keep reserve topics visibly separate. Budget for answers, follow-ups, introductions and a close; do not promise that many unrelated topics fit a short call merely because their question sentences are short. When time is unknown, state a reasonable assumption and identify the first topic to drop. Reuse a working format rather than imposing new columns on every deliverable.

**Written company or FA clarification.** Ask for the exact missing distinction or number, with period/unit when relevant, and accept an existing slide or explanation if it answers the question. A small set of related factual fields can belong together in writing. Avoid sending interview branches, private investment judgments or questions the intermediary cannot reasonably answer; ask them to route a specific issue to the relevant person. Drafting a list does not authorize sending it.

**Review before delivery.** Read the main questions aloud and try plausible answers. If a polished generic answer could satisfy the wording while leaving the key uncertainty untouched, make the question more specific. If a complete answer to its first clause leaves several unrelated tasks, simplify it. Check that known answers have changed this round's questions and that a useful answer can lead to a next step or closure. Review meaning and usability, not a required count or scorecard.

## Worked Contrasts

These are teaching examples, not reusable question templates. The company, events and amounts are invented.

### From An Internal Checklist To A Conversation

Suppose a warehouse scheduling software company reports that its second deployment was faster. The current uncertainty is whether reusable product work reduced effort, or the second site was simply easier.

**Weak:** “请说明产品标准化程度、部署周期、人工投入、定制需求、毛利和未来规模化路径。”

The sentence names everything the analyst wants to understand, but gives the founder no clear place to begin.

**Main question:** “第二个仓库上线时，第一单做过的工作，哪一项这次直接省掉了？”

- If the answer is reusable software or configuration: “有没有遇到两家流程不同、这部分仍然得重做的地方？” This tests the limit of reuse.
- If the answer is that the second site was simpler: “如果下一家和第一家一样复杂，现在还有哪一步能少花人力？” This separates product progress from an easier customer.
- If the answer stays abstract: “拿一个排班规则的修改举例，当时是谁改、怎么交给客户用的？” This returns to an observable task.

**Enough for this screen:** understand what actually repeated and what remained customer-specific, then decide whether a product-lead discussion would add value. Do not require all branches to be asked or treat one deployment as proof of scalable margins.

### From A Desired Answer To A Real Choice

**Weak:** “你们产品效果这么好，客户应该会继续扩容吧？”

**Main question:** “最近一次客户决定不扩大使用范围，是怎么做出这个决定的？”

If a relevant case exists, follow the actual reason. A budget freeze leads to asking what was funded instead; low usage leads to asking where the workflow stopped. If there has been no such decision, use the latest actual scope decision and retain the lack of negative examples as a limit. Do not invent a lost customer to complete the question.

### From Repeating A Fact To Testing Its Meaning

**Already known:** in the fictional prior interview, the company said its first paid customer renewed for one year.

**Weak:** “客户会续费吗？复购率怎么样？”

**Next question:** “这次续约时，客户最想改掉原来方案里的哪一点？”

If nothing changed, ask what made keeping the existing arrangement worthwhile. The purpose is to learn what the customer values and what almost prevented renewal, not to re-collect the fact that renewal happened. Attribute the answer to the company unless heard from the customer.

### A Written Clarification Can Be Simpler

**Weak:** “请提供完整收入、客户、合同和回款明细，以证明商业化质量。”

**Written request:** “材料中的‘本季度收入 240 万元’是否包含尚未确认收入的订单？请按目前财务口径解释即可；若已有对应页，可直接标出。”

This resolves a specific, decision-relevant ambiguity without demanding a new data room. If the numbers were already clear, there would be no reason to ask it.
